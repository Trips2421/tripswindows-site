Historic & hillside glass specialists

Los Feliz homes span a century of architecture, and each era demands different window care.

From the landmark Ennis House to the Spanish Revival estates along Glendower Avenue and the Schindler-influenced homes tucked into Franklin Hills, Los Feliz contains one of the most architecturally diverse collections of residential glass in Los Angeles. Leaded casement windows on 1920s mansions require delicate handling. Floor-to-ceiling panels on mid-century builds require specialized squeegee work and careful edging around original hardware.

Griffith Park creates a unique cleaning challenge. Coast live oaks, California sycamores, and chaparral shed pollen, sap, and fine particulate that settles on hillside glass within weeks. Fire season compounds the problem with ash deposits that etch glass if left untreated. Quarterly cleaning is standard for most Los Feliz properties, with hillside estates and glass-intensive homes benefiting from more frequent visits.

Los Feliz cleaning schedule

How often should you clean windows in Los Feliz?

Frequency depends on hillside exposure and proximity to Griffith Park.

Standard Los Feliz homes

Quarterly service (winter, spring, summer, fall). Griffith Park pollen and dust accumulate faster on hillside glass than in the flats. Four cleanings per year keeps views of the Observatory and downtown sharp.

Mid-century glass-intensive homes

Quarterly to bi-monthly for Neutra, Schindler, and other mid-century properties where walls of glass are the defining feature. When the architecture is designed around the view, hazy glass defeats the purpose.

Hillside estates & park-adjacent

Three to four times yearly for homes backing directly onto Griffith Park. Fire season brings ash and particulate that demands prompt attention, and heavy tree canopy means year-round organic debris.

Historic homes with leaded glass

Twice yearly at minimum for Spanish Colonial and early Hollywood-era homes. Regular cleaning prevents mineral deposits from etching vintage glass and keeps leaded panes looking their best.
