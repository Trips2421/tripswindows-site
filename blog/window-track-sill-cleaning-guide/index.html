Window Track and Sill Cleaning: The Step Most People Skip

2026-07-09 · 7 min read

Everyone notices clean glass. Almost nobody thinks about the tracks and sills underneath, until a window won't slide, water pools on the frame, or a line of black grime appears in the channel. Tracks and sills are where dirt, dead bugs, and grit quietly collect, and cleaning them is part of a genuinely thorough window cleaning. Here's how to do it right and why it matters.

How do I clean window tracks and sills?

Clean window tracks by first vacuuming out the loose debris, then loosening stuck-on grime with a brush and a little soapy water or a vinegar solution, wiping it out with a cloth, and drying the channel completely. A stiff detail brush or an old toothbrush gets into the corners where dirt cakes hardest, and cotton swabs handle the very ends of the track.

Do the sill at the same time with the same solution and cloth. Finish by checking that the small drainage openings at the front of the track are clear, which is the step most people miss.

Why do window tracks get so dirty?

Window tracks get dirty because they're a horizontal channel right at the opening of the house, so they catch everything: outdoor dust, pollen, insect debris, and, in LA, marine-layer moisture and sprinkler overspray that turn the dust into a grimy paste. Every time the window opens, more gets pulled in.

Because tracks are out of sight, the buildup goes unnoticed until it's significant, or until it starts affecting how the window works.

What happens if you never clean window tracks?

If tracks are never cleaned, three things tend to happen: the window gets harder to open and close as grit builds up in the channel, the small drainage holes clog so rainwater backs up onto the frame instead of draining away, and trapped moisture plus organic debris can grow mold and mildew. Over years, standing water in a neglected track can even damage the frame.

None of it is dramatic on any single day, which is exactly why it gets ignored, and why it's worth catching before it becomes a repair.

Why won't my window slide smoothly anymore?

A window that's gotten stiff or gritty to slide is usually telling you the track is packed with debris, not that the window is broken. Dirt, hardened grime, and small objects in the channel create friction and can jam the rollers on sliding windows and doors.

A thorough track cleaning fixes most sticking windows on its own. If it still binds after the channel is clean and clear, the hardware or rollers may need attention, but clean the track first, it solves the problem more often than not.

What are weep holes and why do they matter?

Weep holes are the small slots or openings at the front edge of a window track that let rainwater drain out of the channel to the exterior. They matter because when they clog with debris, water has nowhere to go, it pools in the track and can eventually seep toward the interior or the wall.

Keeping weep holes clear is one of the most important and most overlooked parts of window maintenance, especially heading into LA's rainy season. A clear track with clear weep holes drains exactly as designed.

Do professional window cleaners clean the tracks too?

A thorough professional cleaning includes the tracks and sills, not just the glass, though it's worth confirming, since some quick services do glass only. At Trip's, detailing the tracks and wiping the sills is part of a complete window cleaning, because clean glass over a filthy track never looks truly finished.

When you book, it helps to mention if the tracks are especially bad so the crew can plan for it, the same kind of heads-up we suggest in our guide on how to prepare for a window cleaning appointment.

How often should window tracks be cleaned?

For most LA homes, cleaning the tracks and sills a couple of times a year keeps them functioning and prevents buildup, ideally as part of the same visit as a full window cleaning. Homes near the coast, near heavy landscaping, or with sprinkler overspray may want it more often, since those conditions load the tracks faster.

Pairing track cleaning with your regular glass cleaning is the easiest way to stay ahead of it, and it's built into what happens during a full visit, as described in what to expect during a professional window cleaning.

Can dirty tracks cause leaks or mold?

Yes, dirty tracks are a common hidden cause of both. Clogged weep holes let water pool and back up, which can lead to leaks onto the sill or into the wall, and the combination of trapped moisture and organic debris in a shaded channel is ideal for mold and mildew.

Keeping tracks clean and draining is a small maintenance habit that prevents a genuinely annoying and sometimes damaging problem, and it protects the window frame and the wall around it at the same time.
