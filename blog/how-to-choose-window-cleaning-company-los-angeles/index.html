How to Choose a Window Cleaning Company in Los Angeles

2026-06-22 · 7 min read

Hiring someone to clean your windows sounds simple, but the gap between a careful, insured crew and a rushed, uninsured one is wide — and it usually isn't visible until something goes wrong. Letting a stranger onto ladders around your home is a small act of trust, and a little vetting up front protects your property and your time. This guide walks through exactly what to look for, the questions worth asking, and the red flags that should make you pause.

What should I look for when choosing a window cleaning company in Los Angeles?

When choosing a window cleaning company in Los Angeles, look for five things:

1. Insurance. Proof of general liability coverage and workers' compensation. 2. A local track record. An established presence with verifiable, recent reviews. 3. Clear written quotes. Documents that spell out exactly what's included. 4. Trained employees rather than rotating day labor. 5. A real assessment — willingness to look at the property (in person or in detail) before quoting.

A company that checks all five is far more likely to do careful work, stand behind it, and protect you if something goes wrong. The rest of this guide unpacks each one.

Should a window cleaner be licensed and insured in California?

Insurance is the non-negotiable one. Any window cleaner working on your property should carry general liability insurance — which covers accidental damage to your home, like a cracked pane or a knocked-over fixture — and workers' compensation, so you aren't held liable if a worker is injured on your property.

On licensing: in California, window cleaning itself doesn't require a specialized state contractor's license the way construction trades do, but the business should hold a valid city business license. The practical move is simple — ask for a certificate of insurance, and don't accept "we're covered" as an answer. A legitimate company sends proof without hesitation. We touch on why this matters for safety in our piece on why DIY window cleaning often isn't worth the risk; the same logic applies to who you let onto a ladder at your home.

What questions should I ask before hiring a window cleaner?

A short list of questions tells you as much about professionalism as the price does:

Are you insured, and can you send a certificate? Do you use your own trained employees or subcontractors? What exactly does the quote include — interior, exterior, screens, tracks, and sills? How do you handle hard-to-reach or second-story windows safely? What happens if a window is scratched or a screen is damaged? Do you guarantee the work if streaks appear afterward? And how do you handle scheduling and weather?

You're not looking for any single magic answer — you're listening for confidence, specifics, and a willingness to put things in writing. Vague or annoyed responses are themselves an answer.

How do I compare window cleaning quotes fairly?

Compare quotes on scope, not just the bottom-line number. This is where most people go wrong: one quote may cover only exterior glass while another includes interior glass, screens, tracks, and sills. The higher figure can easily be the better value once you line up what's actually included.

Confirm each quote lists the number and type of windows, whether screens and tracks are part of the job, how multi-story access is handled, and whether there's a satisfaction guarantee. A clear, itemized quote from a company that took the time to assess the property is far more reliable than a vague flat number quoted sight-unseen. If you want a sense of what a thorough visit actually covers, our overview of what to expect during professional window cleaning is a useful benchmark, as is the distinction between interior and exterior cleaning.

What are the red flags of a bad window cleaning company?

A few warning signs should make you slow down:

No proof of insurance, or evasiveness when you ask for it. Pressure to pay the full amount in cash up front. A quote given over the phone with no questions about your home. No verifiable reviews or local presence. No written confirmation of what's included. And door-to-door solicitation pushing a "today only" deal.

Any one of these is reason for caution. The combination of vague scope, cash-only payment, and no insurance is the classic profile of a job that goes sideways — and because there's no insurance and no paper trail, you have little recourse when it does.

Do online reviews matter when picking a window cleaner?

Yes, but read them for substance, not just the star rating. Look for reviews that mention specifics — punctuality, how the crew handled tricky access, how the company responded if something needed a touch-up. A consistent pattern of detailed, recent reviews from a real local customer base means far more than a high average built on a handful of vague comments.

It's also worth checking whether the company actually responds to reviews. Engagement after the job is done is a quiet signal that they care about the relationship, not just the transaction.

Is the cheapest quote usually the best choice?

Rarely. The lowest quote often reflects a narrower scope (exterior only), uninsured labor, or a rushed visit that skips screens, tracks, and detail work. Those shortcuts resurface later as redone work — or, worse, as property damage you can't recover because the company wasn't insured.

The better comparison is value: what's included, who's doing the work, whether they're insured, and whether they guarantee the result. A fair quote from an established, insured company that does thorough work protects both your home and your time. Price matters, but it's one factor among several — not the whole decision.

Does it matter if a company uses employees or day labor?

It matters a lot. Companies that use their own trained, vetted employees tend to deliver more consistent results, take more care with your property, and carry the workers' compensation coverage that protects you. Rotating day labor or subcontractors is cheaper for the company but introduces inconsistency — and liability questions. If an uninsured worker is hurt on your property, you can end up exposed.

Ask directly: who will be doing the work, and are they employees of the company I'm hiring? At Trip's Windows, we're a family-run team serving LA since 2020 — the same trained crew, not a revolving roster, which is a big part of why our work stays consistent across every neighborhood we serve.

How often should I have my windows professionally cleaned?

For most Los Angeles homes, twice a year is a sensible baseline. Coastal properties exposed to marine-layer salt and homes under heavy tree canopy or in dusty hillside areas do better on a quarterly schedule, and commercial storefronts often need monthly or biweekly service to keep up appearances.
