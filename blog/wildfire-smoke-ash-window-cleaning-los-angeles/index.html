Wildfire Smoke and Ash on Windows: How to Clean It Safely

2026-07-09 · 8 min read

After a wildfire event anywhere near Los Angeles, a fine layer of ash settles on everything, and windows and screens take the brunt of it. The instinct is to grab a cloth and wipe it away, but ash is one of the worst things you can dry-wipe across glass. Here's how to deal with smoke and ash safely, protect yourself while you do it, and know when it's a job worth handing off.

How do I clean wildfire ash off my windows?

Clean wildfire ash off windows by rinsing first, not wiping. Gently flood the glass with water (a hose on low, or plenty of water from a bottle) to float the ash away before it can be dragged across the surface, then follow with a normal gentle wash using a soft cloth or squeegee and a mild solution. The key is to lift the ash off with water rather than grind it into the glass with a dry cloth.

Work top to bottom, re-rinse as needed, and be patient, ash cleanup is a two-step process of rinse, then clean, precisely because the particles are abrasive.

Why shouldn't I wipe ash off dry?

You shouldn't wipe ash off dry because ash is gritty and mildly abrasive, and dragging it across glass with a dry or barely damp cloth can leave fine scratches that never come out. Those micro-scratches dull the glass and become more visible over time, especially in direct sun.

The same caution applies to painted surfaces, car finishes, and screens. Always float ash off with water first, the extra step is what protects the surface underneath.

Is wildfire ash dangerous to handle?

Wildfire ash can be irritating and is best treated as hazardous, because it can contain fine particulates and residues from whatever burned, not just wood. When cleaning up ash, it's wise to wear an N95 mask, gloves, long sleeves, and eye protection, avoid stirring it into the air, and keep kids and pets away from the area until it's cleared.

If you have respiratory sensitivities, take extra care or leave the exterior cleanup to someone equipped for it. This is a real safety consideration, not just a cleaning preference.

Does ash and smoke residue damage windows and screens?

Yes, if it's left in place. Ash is slightly alkaline and, combined with moisture from marine layer or dew, can etch glass and corrode metal window frames and screen mesh over time. Smoke residue also leaves an oily film that bonds more stubbornly the longer it sits.

Prompt, gentle cleaning prevents most of this. The damage comes from letting ash and smoke film sit through days of humidity cycles, which is common when people focus on interiors first and forget the exterior glass and screens.

Should I clean screens after a fire or replace them?

Start by cleaning, most screens can be saved by removing them, rinsing thoroughly, and gently washing the mesh, but heavily ash-clogged or corroded screens sometimes need replacement. Fiberglass mesh that's become brittle or aluminum mesh showing corrosion is usually worth replacing rather than repeatedly cleaning.

If you're unsure which way to go, our guide on screen repair versus replacement lays out the decision, and our screen repair service can re-mesh frames that are structurally fine but too far gone to clean.

How do I clean smoke film off the inside of windows?

Smoke film on interior glass is an oily residue rather than dry ash, so it responds to a gentle degreasing approach: a mild ammonia-free cleaner or a drop of dish soap in warm water, applied with a soft microfiber cloth, then buffed dry. It often takes two passes, one to cut the film and one to polish.

Avoid abrasive pads, which can scratch, and be gentle on any tinted or Low-E glass, as we cover in our guide to cleaning Low-E and tinted windows. Smoke film can also settle on sills and tracks, so those are worth wiping at the same time.

Do I need to clean solar panels after a wildfire?

Yes, ash settling on solar panels blocks light and drops output, and like on glass, it shouldn't be wiped dry. Panels should be gently rinsed and cleaned with proper technique, ideally when they're cool and ideally by someone comfortable working safely on a roof.

Ash is one of the fastest ways to lose panel efficiency, on top of LA's normal dust, as we explain in our guide on how often to clean solar panels in LA. After a significant ash event, a panel cleaning is well worth it.

When should I hire a professional after a fire?

Hire a professional when there's heavy ash across the whole exterior, second-story or rooftop glass and panels involved, delicate or specialty glass at stake, or simply when you'd rather not handle potentially hazardous ash yourself. Professionals bring the water-first method, safe access, and protective equipment to do it thoroughly without scratching surfaces or risking your health.
