Fall Exterior Maintenance Checklist for Los Angeles Homes

2026-07-23 · 8 min read

LA doesn't have a dramatic fall, but it does have a rainy season — and the weeks before it arrive are the most important maintenance window of the year. A summer's worth of dust, marine residue, and organic growth has built up on your home, and the first storms are what turn deferred maintenance into real damage. This checklist walks through what to handle in early fall, in the order that matters, so your home is ready before the weather turns.

What should be on a fall home maintenance checklist in Los Angeles?

A fall home maintenance checklist for Los Angeles should cover five things: clean the gutters before the first rains, wash off the summer's accumulated dust and marine-layer residue from windows, pressure wash hardscape to clear buildup and slippery growth, inspect and soft wash the roof, and repair or replace damaged window screens.

LA doesn't get harsh winters, but the fall rainy season is exactly when deferred exterior maintenance turns into water damage, drainage problems, and slip hazards. Fall is the window to get ahead of all of it in one coordinated push. Here's each item in detail.

When should I clean my gutters in Los Angeles for the rainy season?

Clean your gutters in September or October, before the first significant rains arrive — typically late fall through winter in LA. Gutters fill through summer and fall with dry leaves, dust, jacaranda and sycamore debris, and windblown material. If they're clogged when the first storm hits, water overflows the edges and can damage fascia, siding, foundations, and landscaping.

Getting them cleared in early fall means the system is ready to move water the moment the weather turns. Our guide on how often to clean gutters in Southern California covers the full cadence, and gutter cleaning is the single most important item on this list for protecting the structure.

Why is fall a good time to clean windows in LA?

Fall is an ideal time to clean windows in LA because your glass has absorbed a full summer of dust, pollen, dry-season grime, and — near the coast — marine-layer residue. As the days get shorter and the light comes in at a lower angle, that accumulated film becomes much more visible on the glass.

A fall cleaning resets the windows before the holidays and before winter rains streak the dust into stubborn spotting. It's also comfortable working weather. If your glass is already showing spots from summer irrigation, that's worth addressing now too — see how to handle hard water staining on glass.

Should I pressure wash my driveway and patio in the fall?

Yes — fall is a smart time to pressure wash driveways, walkways, and patios in LA. Summer bakes in oil, dust, and grime, and shaded areas can grow a thin layer of algae or mildew that becomes genuinely slippery once the rains start. Clearing hardscape in fall removes the summer buildup and eliminates that slip hazard before wet weather.

It also stops organic growth from embedding deeper into porous concrete and stone through the damp months. Knowing which surfaces get pressure washed versus soft washed matters here — hardscape takes pressure, but delicate surfaces don't. Pressure washing the driveway and patio is a quick fall win.

Do I need to prepare my roof for the rainy season?

Yes. Before the rainy season, clear any accumulated leaves and debris from the roof and valleys, and have moss, lichen, or black algae streaks soft washed off. Debris and organic growth trap moisture against the roofing, which accelerates wear and can contribute to leaks once it's raining regularly.

Roofs should be soft washed, never pressure washed, because high pressure strips protective granules and can force water under the roofing. Pairing a roof soft wash with gutter cleaning is the most efficient fall roof prep — both address the same goal of keeping water moving off and away from the house.

What happens if I skip gutter cleaning before winter?

Skipping gutter cleaning before winter is one of the most common causes of avoidable rainy-season damage in LA. Clogged gutters overflow during storms, sending water down exterior walls and pooling near the foundation. That leads to fascia rot, stained siding, crawlspace moisture, eroded landscaping, and — on hillside lots — drainage problems that undermine slopes.

A single fall cleaning is far easier than repairing the water damage that clogged gutters cause over a wet winter. The warning signs are usually visible before the first storm; our post on 5 signs your gutters need cleaning covers what to look for.

How is fall maintenance different for hillside and canyon homes?

Hillside and canyon homes need extra fall attention to drainage because they shed water across slopes rather than flat lots. Gutters, downspouts, and drainage channels have to be completely clear so winter runoff moves away from the structure instead of pooling or undermining the hillside. Heavy tree canopy in canyon neighborhoods also drops more debris into gutters.

For these properties, fall gutter and drainage service isn't just cosmetic — it's part of protecting the home from erosion and water intrusion. Homes in Bel Air, Brentwood, Pacific Palisades, and the canyon neighborhoods should treat drainage as the top fall priority, as we detail in our hillside home maintenance guide.

When should I book fall exterior maintenance in Los Angeles?

Book fall exterior maintenance in September or early October, before the first rains and before the pre-holiday rush. Scheduling early means gutters are clear when storms arrive, hardscape is safe before it gets wet, and windows are clean for both the lower fall light and holiday gatherings.

Fall books up quickly because most homeowners want the same pre-rain, pre-holiday window, so reserving a few weeks ahead ensures you get the timing you want. Our guide to the best time of year for exterior maintenance puts the fall window in the context of the full LA calendar.

Can I get all my fall exterior maintenance done in one visit?

Yes — window cleaning, gutter cleaning, pressure washing, roof soft washing, and screen repair can typically be coordinated into a single visit or a tightly scheduled sequence. Bundling them is more efficient than booking separate appointments, and it lets the crew run the services in the right order (for example, pressure washing and roof work before window cleaning so debris doesn't re-deposit on fresh glass).

One coordinated fall visit gets the whole property ready for winter at once — and it's the easiest way to make sure nothing on the checklist slips through.
