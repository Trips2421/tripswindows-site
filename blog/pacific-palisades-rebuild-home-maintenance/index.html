Pacific Palisades Is Rebuilding: How to Protect Your New Home's Exterior

May 11, 2026 · 3 min read

The Pacific Palisades community has shown incredible resilience since the January 2025 fire. Over a year later, the neighborhood is deep into its rebuild — new homes are going up, foundations are being poured, and families are starting to move back in. It's an inspiring thing to see.

But here's something that catches a lot of new homeowners off guard: a brand-new home in the middle of an active construction zone gets dirty remarkably fast. And if you don't stay on top of it early, some of that buildup can cause real damage to your new surfaces.

Post-construction film on new windows

If you've recently moved into a newly built home in the Palisades, your windows probably have a hazy film on them — even if they look clean at first glance. That's a combination of construction dust, stucco overspray, paint mist, and adhesive residue from manufacturer labels. Standard glass cleaner won't cut through it.

Professional post-construction window cleaning uses specialized techniques to remove that film without scratching the glass. This is especially important for coated or low-E glass, which is standard in most new builds. Getting it done right the first time sets the baseline for how your windows will look going forward.

Hard water stains start sooner than you think

New construction sites run a lot of water — irrigation being installed, concrete being cured, landscapers getting plants established. Sprinkler overspray hitting fresh glass is one of the fastest ways to etch permanent hard water stains into new windows. Once those mineral deposits bond to the glass, they're extremely difficult to remove.

The best move is to get your windows professionally cleaned within the first few weeks of moving in, and then stay on a regular schedule. Catching hard water spots early — before they bake into the surface — is the difference between a quick cleaning and a restoration job.

Gutters fill up fast without established landscaping

Here's one that's specific to the rebuild: most new homes in the Palisades don't have mature landscaping yet. That means exposed hillside soil, freshly graded lots next door, and construction debris blowing around the neighborhood. All of that ends up in your gutters.

Without established ground cover and root systems to hold the soil in place, even a light rain can wash sediment straight into your gutter system. Clogged gutters on a hillside home are a serious issue — water that can't drain properly will find its own path, and that usually means into your foundation or down the slope. Regular gutter cleaning during the rebuild phase is essential, even if your home is brand new.

Construction dust settles on everything

New driveways, walkways, and patios look great on move-in day. A few weeks later, they're coated in a layer of fine construction dust from the active building sites nearby. Concrete dust, drywall particulate, and general debris settle on hardscapes daily in an active rebuild zone.

Periodic pressure washing keeps those surfaces clean and prevents grit from grinding into the finish. For homes with new pavers or stamped concrete, this is especially important — embedded dust and dirt can discolor sealant and age the surface prematurely.

Homes that survived need extra attention too

Not every home in the Palisades was lost in the fire. If yours made it through, you're likely dealing with a different version of the same problem — months of heavy construction activity on surrounding lots sending dust, debris, and runoff your way. The maintenance needs are higher than normal right now, even for established homes with mature landscaping.

Whether your home is brand new or one of the ones that held strong, staying ahead of the mess is the smartest thing you can do during the rebuild phase.
