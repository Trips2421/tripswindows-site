How to Clean Hard Water Stains Off Glass Doors

April 29, 2026 · 3 min read

If you live in Los Angeles, you know the struggle: hard water stains on glass shower doors, patio doors, and windows that seem impossible to remove. Those unsightly white, cloudy deposits aren't dirt—they're mineral buildup from our region's notoriously hard water. The good news? You can tackle them. Here's what you need to know.

What Causes Hard Water Stains?

Los Angeles has some of the hardest water in the country. When water with high mineral content (calcium and magnesium) dries on glass, it leaves behind mineral deposits that bond to the surface. This happens on shower doors, patio doors, windows, and anywhere water sits and evaporates—especially from sprinklers on exterior glass.

Over time, these stains accumulate and can actually etch into the glass, making them harder to remove. The longer you wait, the more stubborn the problem becomes.

DIY Solutions for Light to Moderate Stains

For fresh or light hard water stains, you can try these budget-friendly approaches:

White Vinegar Solution: Mix equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle. Spray the affected area generously, let it sit for 15-30 minutes, then wipe with a soft cloth or squeegee. The acidity in vinegar dissolves mineral deposits naturally.

Baking Soda Paste: For stubborn spots, make a paste of baking soda and water. Apply it to the stain, let it sit for 10 minutes, then gently scrub with a non-abrasive sponge. Rinse thoroughly with clean water.

Lemon Juice: Fresh lemon juice works similarly to vinegar. Cut a lemon in half and rub it directly on the stain, or spray bottled lemon juice for larger areas.

These methods work best when stains are relatively new and haven't etched into the glass.

Prevention Tips

After your glass is clean, minimize future stains by:

Squeegee shower doors daily to prevent water spots from drying on the surface. Use a water softener if you have one installed. Rinse patio doors and exterior glass after watering your plants. Consider applying a water-repellent treatment to make future cleaning easier.
