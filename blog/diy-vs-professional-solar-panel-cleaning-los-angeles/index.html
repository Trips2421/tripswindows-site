DIY vs Professional Solar Panel Cleaning: When to Hire Help

2026-06-08 · 9 min read

'Can I just clean my solar panels myself?' is one of the most common questions LA solar owners ask. The honest answer is: it depends on your specific situation. This guide breaks down when DIY actually makes sense, when professional service is the right call, and what's at stake in each direction. The decision matters because doing it wrong can cost you both efficiency and warranty coverage.

Should I clean my own solar panels or hire a professional?

Most LA homeowners are better off hiring a professional for solar panel cleaning.

The math favors DIY only for small accessible ground-mount systems or single-story flat-roof installations where you already have safe roof access, the right brushes, and a deionized water source. For multi-story roofs, complex arrays, panels under warranty, or any situation involving climbing ladders or walking on a sloped roof, professional service is the right call.

The cost of professional cleaning is far less than a single fall, panel damage from improper cleaning, or warranty voidance.

Is it safe to clean solar panels yourself?

Cleaning solar panels yourself is safe only under specific conditions.

Safe conditions: single-story building with a flat roof, sturdy roof access without ladder climbing on the building exterior, panels reachable from the roof edge without walking on the panels themselves, and no overhead power lines near the work area.

Unsafe conditions: any pitched roof, any multi-story building, panels that require walking between or stepping on them, and any installation near electrical hazards. Solar panels generate DC current — wet panels in direct sunlight can produce dangerous voltages.

If you're not confident the conditions are safe, they probably aren't. Falls from residential roofs are the leading cause of home-maintenance-related serious injury.

What equipment do I need to clean solar panels safely?

DIY solar panel cleaning requires specific equipment:

Soft-bristle solar panel brush rated for photovoltaic surfaces — standard household brushes can scratch coatings.

Deionized or distilled water in sufficient quantity. Tap water defeats the purpose by leaving mineral residue.

Extendable handle that allows cleaning from the roof edge — not from on top of panels.

Proper fall protection if working at height.

Pressure washers and household cleaning chemicals should not be used. The equipment investment for safe DIY work often approaches the cost of one or two professional cleaning visits. If you don't already own most of this equipment, professional service typically wins on cost alone.

Can I use a pressure washer on solar panels?

Pressure washers should not be used on solar panels.

Manufacturer recommendations consistently cap acceptable pressure around 1,500 PSI; most residential pressure washers operate at 2,000-3,000 PSI. Higher pressure can damage panel surface coatings, force water past sealed edges into the panel interior, and stress the frame and mounting hardware.

Pressure damage often voids the manufacturer warranty. Professional cleaning uses water-fed pole systems that deliver appropriate pressure and pure water without the risks.

Will cleaning my solar panels void the warranty?

Cleaning your solar panels yourself can void the warranty if you use inappropriate methods, but properly performed DIY cleaning generally does not.

Warranty-voiding mistakes include:

Pressure washers above manufacturer-rated PSI.
Abrasive brushes or pads that scratch the surface coating.
Acidic or harsh cleaning chemicals not approved by the manufacturer.
Walking on the panels themselves, cracking cells or damaging frames.
Tap water that leaves mineral residue requiring repeated cleaning (which itself causes wear).

Professional services follow manufacturer protocols and document the cleaning for warranty claims if ever needed.

When is professional solar cleaning definitely worth it?

Professional solar cleaning is definitely worth it when any of these apply:

1. The system is on a pitched or multi-story roof.
2. Panels haven't been cleaned in 12+ months and require restoration rather than maintenance.
3. The system is still under manufacturer warranty (which most are for 20-25 years).
4. The array has known bird-droppings or established mineral staining.
5. Output measurements show 10%+ degradation from baseline.

In any of these cases, the professional cost is recovered in restored production within a few months. See why dirty solar panels lose 15-25% efficiency for the full ROI math.

How long does professional solar cleaning take?

Professional solar panel cleaning for a typical LA residential system takes 30-90 minutes.

A 5-10 kW residential array on a single-roof installation usually takes 30-60 minutes for both the cleaning and a quick output verification check. Larger residential systems with multiple roof sections or commercial arrays take 1-3 hours. The work is fast because professional crews bring water-fed pole systems, deionized water tanks, and trained technique that doesn't require walking on panels.

Most LA homeowners book solar cleaning alongside window cleaning visits for efficient single-trip service.

Can solar cleaners check for other system issues?

Yes — professional solar cleaners typically inspect for visible system issues during the cleaning visit.

Common items flagged: cracked cells or panel surfaces, damaged mounting hardware or rooftop integration, animal nests or debris accumulation around the inverter, wiring damage from sun exposure or weather, and new shading from tree growth or nearby construction.

We don't repair electrical issues but we do flag them so the homeowner can call their installer or electrician. This adds value beyond just cleaning.

How does professional cleaning produce better results than DIY?

Professional solar cleaning produces better results than DIY for three reasons:

Deionized water — no mineral residue means no spotting or efficiency-reducing film. This is the single biggest difference and is hard to replicate with DIY equipment.

Appropriate equipment — soft-bristle brushes, water-fed poles, proper pressure. Most DIY cleaning uses tools designed for cars, decks, or windows that don't work optimally on solar surfaces.

Trained technique — working from around the array rather than on it, addressing different orientations differently, spotting damage during the work.

DIY cleaning with tap water and household tools often deposits more residue than it removes, leaving panels actually less efficient than before. For the common DIY mistakes that cause this, see our streaky windows guide — the same chemistry applies to solar surfaces.

What's the difference between solar panel cleaning and window cleaning?

Solar panel cleaning and window cleaning use similar core methods — deionized water, soft-bristle work, no harsh chemicals — but adapt to different conditions.

Solar panels typically have tougher surface coatings designed for harsh outdoor exposure; the cleaning emphasis is removing efficiency-reducing residue without damaging anti-reflective coatings.

Windows emphasize streak-free finish and edge detail.

Both benefit from water-fed pole systems and trained technique. Many LA homeowners book both services in the same visit to save time and coordinate sequencing — see our summer prep guide for how multi-service visits work.
